# CSDATA Feature Guide

[Back to introduction](README.en.md) · [Visit CSDATA](https://cs2monitor.cn) · [FAQ](FAQ.en.md)

CSDATA connects market overviews, item records, and public inventory monitoring. This guide explains what each module is for. Available content and query ranges depend on the current website and your account's entitlements.

## Market overview: find where activity has changed

The homepage brings together trend charts, category rankings, and recent records to help you identify where changes are happening.

- **Activity trends:** Daily counts of records showing increases or decreases in buy orders and sale listings.
- **Category rankings:** Compare activity across item types to find categories worth a closer look.
- **Recent activity feed:** Browse summaries of item changes, signals, and monitored inventory changes from the past 24 hours.
- **Inventory activity rankings:** See recent inventory activity among the accounts included in monitoring.

These record counts indicate observed activity. They do not represent the number or volume of completed trades.

## Item activity: follow changes over time

View changes between successive valid observations, including buy-order counts, sale-listing counts, lowest listed prices, and price trends.

For a closer look, open the detail and history pages and use the available keyword, date, or other filters. Check the source platform, collection time, and direction of change together; data from different sources may use different definitions.

## Market signals: find records worth checking

Signals are generated by predefined rules applied to collected price and quantity changes. Check the signal type, generation time, and associated item, then compare them with activity records and current market conditions.

A signal describes a rule being triggered. It does not establish a market participant's intent or constitute an automated trading instruction.

## Public inventory monitoring

The website uses the term “大商” for accounts whose public Steam inventories are included in monitoring. This is a selected set of accounts, not coverage of every market participant or a certification of an account's identity or qualifications.

| Page or feature | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Inventory list | View the latest successfully collected inventory snapshot |
| Recent changes | See items added or removed between successive valid snapshots |
| Historical changes | Review inventory changes over a period of time |
| Custom monitoring | Add public inventories you want to follow, subject to your account's entitlements |

Custom monitoring targets are visible to you and site administrators. They are not automatically added to the public monitoring list or public broadcasts. Steam privacy settings, access restrictions, or collection failures may cause temporary gaps in the records.

## Interface and help

- **Desktop and mobile layouts:** Dashboards and navigation adapt to the screen size.
- **Light and dark themes:** Choose the theme that suits your reading preferences.
- **Last successful page load:** See when the page last loaded successfully and refresh your browser to fetch data again. Page load time is separate from the collection times of the underlying records.
- **Help center:** Find getting-started guidance, indicator definitions, and answers to usage questions.
- **Account area:** Check your current entitlements and plan information.

**Website language:** The interface is currently primarily in Chinese. An English-language version of the website is planned for a future update. A release date has not been announced.

## What can I view for free?

Basic access includes the homepage overview and selected previews. Item-related access, monitored-account access, and custom-monitoring access each control their corresponding features.

Availability of full history, detailed signals, monitored-account names, and other restricted content depends on the website's current plan descriptions and the “我的权益” (My entitlements) page. This guide does not list fixed prices or quotas, so it does not become misleading when the service changes.
